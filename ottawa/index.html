The three Brévant selections

Ottawa

For a capital defined by other kinds of business, Ottawa keeps a table more singular than its reputation suggests.

Antheia

Little Italy

Briana Kim runs Antheia as much as a laboratory as a restaurant: sixteen seats arranged around a working fermentation lab, where the kitchen's misos, garums and shoyus age in cedar and in onggi, the tapered clay vessels Korean cooks have used for millennia. The vegetarian tasting menu follows the rhythm of those ferments rather than the calendar, so a visit turns on whatever has reached its peak — a pine-smoked tea broth, a preserved-fig condiment, a dish built around a single cultured grain. It is deliberately small and studious, reached by two flights of stairs, and unlike anything else in the city. Kim's cooking asks for attention and repays it.

Atelier

Little Italy

Atelier has spent well over a decade arguing that Ottawa belongs in any conversation about Canadian fine dining. Marc Lepine cooks a long tasting menu in the avant-garde tradition — precise, technically restless, fond of the tools and textures of modern gastronomy — served with no à la carte fallback in a spare room on Rochester Street. Courses arrive in quick, surprising succession, closer to a sequence of ideas than a conventional dinner. It's a destination for people who want cooking that experiments in earnest rather than for effect, and it has held that line long enough to become an institution on its own terms.

Stofa

Wellington West

Jason Sawision's Stofa holds the friendly middle ground between neighbourhood restaurant and serious kitchen, which is harder than it sounds. The format is a three- or five-course table d'hôte that changes often and roams widely for its ideas, landing on dishes that are technically sharp but rarely solemn — a soufflé, a handmade pasta, the occasional bit of theatre like a dessert that shatters at the table. The Wellington West room is warm and unfussy, built for a long evening rather than a special-occasion performance. It's the kind of place a city is lucky to have: ambitious cooking offered without ceremony, from one of the region's most quietly accomplished chefs.
