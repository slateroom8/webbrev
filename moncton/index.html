The three Brévant selections

Moncton

In New Brunswick's Acadian heart, three rooms cooking the region with real intent.

Les Brumes du Coude

Aberdeen Cultural Centre

Les Brumes du Coude cooks in the spirit of a French bistro with an Acadian accent, from an open kitchen in an old schoolroom in the Aberdeen Cultural Centre — wood, brick and a wine list chalked on a blackboard. Owner-chef Michel Savoie writes the menu daily, and the deeply flavourful cooking rewards it: scallop crudo, comforting pastas, larger meat dishes, plates that change with what's good. The room is casual and warm, more neighbourhood favourite than special-occasion showpiece, though the food holds its own against far grander rooms. It is the sort of restaurant a region builds an identity around — rooted, generous, and cooking above its unassuming setting.

Little Louis' Oyster Bar

Collishaw Street

Little Louis' hides up a set of stairs on an unlikely, industrial-edged street, and opens into one of the most polished dining rooms in Atlantic Canada. Chef-owner Pierre A. Richard has been serving refined seafood and steak here for years, anchored by an oyster bar and a wine list that has drawn recognition well beyond the province. The cooking is classic and precise — pristine Atlantic oysters, handmade pasta with butter-poached lobster, expertly grilled cuts — served with the sort of warm, knowledgeable hospitality that keeps regulars loyal. It is the city's benchmark for an occasion, and it has held that position long enough to feel like an institution rather than a contender.

Atelier Tony

Dieppe

Atelier Tony sits just across the line in Dieppe, part of the same Greater Moncton dining scene, and offers fine dining without a trace of stuffiness. The Holden family — whose Tony's Bistro & Pâtisserie has anchored central Moncton for over a decade — built it as a true family-run room, and the small, seasonal menu shows off Atlantic Canadian ingredients right down to herbs and flowers from the sous-chef's garden. Beautifully plated dishes share the menu with burgers and lobster rolls, which is exactly the point: creative and elevated, but approachable. A scallop ceviche with coconut and cucumber captures the register. It is the loosest of the region's serious rooms, and one of the most likeable.
