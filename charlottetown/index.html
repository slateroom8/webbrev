The three Brévant selections

Charlottetown

On an island defined by what it grows and catches, three rooms that cook it well.

Ada Culinary Studio

Kent Street

Ada is less a nightly restaurant than a stage for Chef Adam Loo's cooking — a culinary studio on Kent Street that runs ticketed, storytelling-driven dinners through the year alongside a weekday grab-and-go counter famous for its sandwiches and filled donuts. The evening events are the reason to plan around it: coursed, interactive menus built around Prince Edward Island's producers, where each dish comes with the story of where it's from, often with the farmers themselves in the room. Loo, one of the island's most respected chefs, treats a dinner as a gathering as much as a meal. Check the calendar and book ahead — this is a restaurant you attend by appointment, and it's worth arranging a visit around.

Slaymaker & Nichols

Fitzroy Street

Slaymaker & Nichols is a forty-five-seat restaurant and small boutique inn in the heart of Charlottetown, built around simple, fresh, local ingredients turned into flavourful small plates made for sharing. The mood is upbeat and stylish rather than formal — old-fashioned cocktails, a well-chosen wine list, a room that leans trendy and cozy at once — and the kitchen ranges confidently from island oysters and steak-on-toast to jerk lamb tacos. It's the kind of place that works equally well for a happy-hour bite at the bar or a long dinner, and the rooms upstairs mean you needn't go far afterward. A dependable, characterful anchor of the downtown dining scene.

Abbiocco Ristorante

Kent Street

Abbiocco takes its name from the pleasant drowsiness that follows a big Italian meal, which tells you something about its intentions. On Kent Street, executive chef Alyssa Dignard cooks modern Italian with a focus on simplicity and execution — house-made pasta, wood-fired pizza, a seasonally rotating menu built on both local and imported quality ingredients — matched to an accessible, thoughtfully assembled wine program. The room is contemporary and comfortable, suited equally to a leisurely lunch, a cocktail, or a celebration. It brings Charlottetown a genuinely good Italian kitchen that plays it straight rather than fussy, and does the fundamentals — pasta, pizza, hospitality — well enough to keep the room full.
