The three Brévant selections

Québec City

Within the old city's walls and just beyond them, three deeply Québécois rooms.

Tanière³

Old Québec

Dinner at Tanière³ begins with an access code and an unmarked door, and unfolds through the stone vaults of a seventeenth-century building in Old Québec. What follows is less a meal than a staged reading of the province's boreal terroir — fifteen or more courses moving from river to forest, each tied to a specific place and producer. François-Emmanuel Nicol works almost entirely with Quebec ingredients, foraged and farmed, building dishes of real delicacy from matured fish, wild mushrooms, roots and game. The progression through the rooms is part of the point; you are not eating in one space for the evening but travelling through several. Few restaurants in the country are this fully committed to their own ground.

Kebec Club Privé

Saint-Roch

Kebec Club Privé seats ten people, once a night, around a single table — and turns dinner into something closer to a gathering of near-strangers who leave as something warmer. Cassandre Osterroth and Pierre-Olivier Pelletier, who met in the kitchens of Laurie Raphaël, transformed a former hair salon in Saint-Roch into this supper club, where a tasting menu of roughly ten courses celebrates Quebec with delicate restraint: cod cured in smoked oil, scallops in a herring-bone jus, herbs fresh and dried, a thread of smoke through much of it. The chefs cook and host at once, wandering out to talk between courses. It is an artisanal, deeply personal evening no larger restaurant could replicate.

Le Clan

Old Québec

Stéphane Modat spent years cooking at the Château Frontenac before opening Le Clan in a historic house in Old Québec, its rooms hung with hunting and fishing trophies that telegraph exactly where his cooking looks. The tasting menu — four courses or six — champions the boreal traditions and ingredients of the province: Gaspé Arctic char, Lake Champlain walleye, red deer, game and fish handled with precise, indulgent technique. Nearly everything is sourced within Quebec, much of it from producers Modat names like friends, and the kitchen adapts the menu almost daily around what comes in. It is regional cooking pursued without compromise, and among the most personal fine-dining experiences in the city.
